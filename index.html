APPLICATION

S.A.G.E. In Action

Topic: Ergonomics (Semester 2, Non-CS Students).

Lecturer Designs the Experience

Objective: Students explain basic principles, analyze risks, propose improvements, and evaluate impacts on health & productivity.

In-Class Activity: Advance Organizer

Show two pictures: (1) Incorrect study setup, (2) Correct workstation setup.

Question: "Which one is more productive? Why?"

Trigger Activity: The Mini Case

"Ali, a university student, studies 6 hours a day using a laptop on his bed. After 2 months, he experiences neck and wrist pain."

What is the cause?
What are long-term risks?
What is the solution?

Students Process & Connect Meaning

Students now have the basic concept. Time to level up their analytical thinking.

Digest Activity 1: Risk Analysis

  • Students take a picture of their own study setup.
  • In groups, identify ergonomic risks.
  • Label affected body parts.
  • Propose improvements.

Internalisation & Mindset Shift

Moving beyond "what is ergonomics" to "Do I care? Will I change my habits?"

Master Design Blueprint Prompt

You are an expert instructional designer and a specialist in higher education pedagogy. I am teaching [COURSE NAME] for [LEVEL OF STUDENT]. Topic: [TOPIC] Design a 1–2 hour active learning lesson plan that includes: 1. Clear Intended Learning Outcomes (ILO) aligned with Bloom’s Taxonomy 2. Advance Organizer activity (5–10 minutes) 3. Two Intermittent Active Learning activities (student-centered) 4. One Higher-Order Thinking (HOTS) activity 5. Closure activity (reflection or synthesis) 6. Suggested formative assessment method 7. Real-world application of the topic 8. Possible misconceptions students may have 9. Scaffolding strategies for weak learners 10. Suggested use of AI tools inside class (if relevant) Make the lesson learner-centered, aligned with Constructive Alignment principles, and suitable for the Malaysian higher education context. Present in a structured format.

Deep Concept Breakdown

Act as a cognitive learning coach. For the topic: [TOPIC] Help students digest this topic by: 1. Breaking it into 5 core concepts 2. Explaining relationships between the concepts 3. Showing one real-world example for each concept 4. Generating 5 “why” questions to deepen understanding 5. Identifying common misunderstandings Structure the output so students can see how ideas connect. Avoid oversimplified explanations.
